What is AI visibility?

AI visibility is how often an AI assistant names your business when someone asks it for a recommendation. A person types a question such as “who is the best family law solicitor in Leeds” into ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity, and the assistant answers with two to four firms. If your firm is one of them, you are visible for that question. If it is not, that person never sees you, whatever your Google ranking is.

It is measured as a share. We ask the same set of client questions many times on each assistant and count the answers that name you. A firm named in 15 of 150 answers has 10% visibility. One answer by itself tells you very little, because the same question returns a different list almost every time it is asked.

An AI visibility audit measures that share, shows which competitors are named instead of you, and lists the sources the assistants relied on. A fix sprint then changes what those sources say about your firm.

How many people ask AI for a recommendation?

Enough to matter, and the number is moving fast. Here is what has been measured so far.

of US consumers used generative AI tools to get local business recommendations in the past year. One year earlier it was 6%. Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey, February 2026, 1,002 US adults.

54%

of UK adults now use AI tools such as ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini. A year earlier it was 31%. Source: Ofcom, Adults’ Media Use and Attitudes, April 2026.

66%

of local searches on Google Maps showed the business being tracked. ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews named the same business in 32% to 38% of those searches. Source: BrightLocal local AI visibility study, September 2026, about 200,000 searches.

Read these with care. Three of the four come from the United States, and two were published by companies that sell marketing services. Nobody has yet published good UK data on how many people choose a solicitor or a dentist through an AI assistant. The direction is clear. The size is not.

Why can't you just ask ChatGPT once?

You can, and you should. But one answer is one draw from a hat. SparkToro and Gumshoe had 600 volunteers run the same 12 prompts nearly 3,000 times. The chance of getting the same list of brands twice was under 1 in 100.

BrightLocal found the same thing for local businesses. Repeat a local search on an AI assistant and only 21% to 33% of the businesses named are the ones named last time.

So a screenshot of one answer proves very little, in either direction. What holds steady is how often a business is named across many runs. That share is the number we report.

Sources: SparkToro and Gumshoe, January 2026. BrightLocal, September 2026.

If your firm is regulated

For solicitors in England and Wales, part of this work overlaps with rules you already follow. The SRA Transparency Rules require price and service information for certain services, your complaints procedure, your SRA number and the SRA digital badge on your website. Those are the same pages an assistant reads when someone asks what a conveyancing solicitor costs.

During the sprint we flag anything on those pages that looks missing or out of date. We write in plain English, we make no claim a regulator would call misleading, and nothing is published until someone at the firm has approved it.

The same care applies to dental practices and to mortgage and financial advisers, who have their own advertising rules. We are not a law firm and this is not compliance advice. The decision stays with your compliance officer.
