What is expert content?

Expert content is material that comes from the person who does the work: the solicitor, the dentist or the adviser, speaking from cases they have handled. It answers the questions clients really ask, in that person’s own words, with their name on it and a date that shows when it was last checked.

It is the opposite of the generic article. Any firm can publish “five things to know about probate”, and any AI tool can write it in a minute. Only your partner can say what goes wrong most often in a probate application, what it usually costs at your firm, and what they tell a client on the first call.

The hard part has never been knowledge. It is time. A partner can answer a client question in two minutes of talking and will not find two hours to write the answer down. So we record the talking and do the writing.

Why does it have to come from the partner?

Three reasons, each with a source you can check.

42%

of the citations in local AI answers pointed to the business’s own website, more than any other kind of source. What your site says in its own words is the first thing an assistant reads. Source: BrightLocal local AI visibility study, September 2026, 1.9 million citations, United States.

“Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content? Do pages carry a byline, where one might be expected?”

Google Search Central, questions to ask about your own content

“Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials to make content appear as if it was written by human experts) is a form of deception.”

Google Search Central, the same page, updated 5 October 2026

Google asks three things of any page: who made it, how it was made, and why. It asks whether the content shows first-hand expertise, and it now names invented experts as deception that marks a page as low quality. A real partner with a real byline is the plainest answer to all of that.

The third reason is your regulator. In August 2026 the Solicitors Regulation Authority issued a warning notice on the misuse of AI. It deals with legal work and not with marketing, but its principle is the one we work to: the solicitor stays accountable for anything produced with AI.

Read this with care. No study shows that a partner’s name on a page makes an assistant cite it. The evidence supports something narrower. Assistants lean on firm websites, Google asks who wrote a page, and regulators hold the firm responsible for what it publishes. A real expert, named and checked, meets all three.

What one recorded hour becomes

Everything below comes out of the same 60 minutes. None of it starts from a blank prompt.

4

Answer pages

One page for each question, 600 to 900 words. The short answer comes first, then the detail, then fees or timescales where you publish them. Each page carries the partner’s name, their role and the date it was last reviewed.

8

Short videos

Two for each question, 30 to 60 seconds long, cut from the recording. The partner on camera, captions on screen, in vertical and square formats.

8

Posts

Four for the partner’s LinkedIn profile and four for your Google Business Profile. Each one points to an answer page.

4

Quote cards

One graphic for each question in your firm’s colours, for the feed and for the top of the page.

The rules we work by

The first two are not negotiable. They are the reason the content can carry a partner’s name.

A real person on camera

The partner appears as themselves. No avatars, no cloned voices and no generated headshots.

Nothing goes live without sign-off

Every page, clip and post is approved by the partner and by whoever handles compliance at your firm.

Only what was said

Pages are built from the recording. If we add a fact the partner did not say, it is marked for them to check.

No client details

The questions are general ones. Anything that could identify a client is cut before the first draft.

No ranking promises

We do not promise positions, citations or enquiries. We promise the content, on time and checked.

Everything stays yours

The recordings, pages, clips and posts belong to the firm, with no licence to renew.

If your firm is regulated

For solicitors in England and Wales, the SRA Transparency Rules already require price and service information on your website for certain services. An answer page is a natural home for it, written in the plain English clients search in.

Dental practices in the UK follow the General Dental Council’s guidance on advertising. Health practitioners in Australia cannot use patient testimonials about clinical care under the Ahpra advertising guidelines, which is one more reason the practitioner speaks and patients do not.

Every page goes to your compliance contact before it is published. We are not a law firm and this is not compliance advice. The decision stays with your compliance officer.
