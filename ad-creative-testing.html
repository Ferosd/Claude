What is ad creative testing?

Ad creative testing is running several different ads against the same audience and the same budget to see which idea sells, then putting the money behind the winner. The thing being tested is the concept, which is the reason a stranger should care. One ad opens on the problem. Another shows how the product works. A third compares it with the cheap alternative. Each concept gets a few different opening lines, called hooks, because the opening seconds decide whether anyone watches the rest.

After a set period you compare the concepts on the same measures: how many people watched past three seconds, how many clicked, and what a purchase cost. The winner gets more budget. The losers are switched off, and what they taught you goes into the next batch.

It is not the same as making more ads. Twenty versions of one idea with a new colour or caption tell you almost nothing. Four ideas that are really different tell you what your customers respond to.

What a batch looks like

Every batch starts from four concepts. Each one is a different reason to buy, written as one sentence you can argue with before any video is made.

Each concept then gets three hooks, which are three different ways to open the same idea. That gives twelve video ads. If one concept wins, the hooks tell you which opening carried it.

From the second month the batch also holds four new variations of the previous winner, so the ad that is working gets fresh versions before it wears out.

What the readout tells you

Four numbers for each concept and one decision. Every number comes from your own Ads Manager, so you can check it.

Hook rate
3-second video plays ÷ impressionsWhether the opening stops the scroll. It is the first number to settle, and it needs the least budget.
Hold rate
ThruPlays ÷ 3-second video playsWhether people who stopped kept watching. Meta counts a ThruPlay when a video plays to the end or for at least 15 seconds.
Click rate
Link clicks ÷ impressionsWhether the ad made people want to see the product page.
Cost per purchase
Spend ÷ purchases Meta attributes to the adThe number that pays the bills, and the noisiest one. The readout shows how many purchases it rests on, so a result built on six sales is not read like one built on sixty.

AI-made ads and the rules

An ad made with AI is still an ad, and the existing rules apply to it. In the United States the FTC’s rule on reviews and testimonials has banned fake ones since October 2024, and it names reviews written by AI for people who do not exist.

In the United Kingdom fake reviews have been banned outright since April 2025, and the advertising code requires that an ad does not mislead. In the European Union the transparency rules of the AI Act began to apply in August 2026. They require realistic AI-generated video to be disclosed as such.

In practice that means product shots and demonstrations are the default. If a presenter appears, they speak as the brand and never as a customer. We do not write reviews, invent results or fake a before and after. Where a platform or a law asks for an AI label, the ad carries one.

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. The final call on any claim is yours.
